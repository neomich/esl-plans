What's the "Derren Brown: The Language Barrier" ESL Lesson About?

B1-B2-C1 · General English

Main Objectives

  • analyze the power of nonverbal communication and the Mehrabian 7-38-55 rule through targeted body language vocabulary;
  • critically evaluate a multi-stage psychological experiment to understand how subtle cues and expectations shape perception;
  • explore the real-world implications of human suggestibility and the ethics of psychological manipulation.

About This Lesson

Page 1: The 7-38-55 Rule & Body Language Vocabulary The lesson opens with a thought-provoking quote by Derren Brown about how language can make us feel "unapproachable." Students review essential body language vocabulary (posture, gestures, fidgeting, mirroring, gaze, glance, grimace, smirk) and discuss the famous "7-38-55 Rule," which claims that 93% of communication is nonverbal. They then imagine themselves in cafes in Poland, Paris, or Japan, trying to guess how much of a conversation they could understand without knowing the local language, setting the stage for a deep dive into intuition. Page 2: The "Language Barrier" Experiment (Stage 1) Students watch the first part of Derren Brown’s experiment, "The Language Barrier Is Broken." They meet Basil (who speaks Arabic) and Susan (who speaks Hungarian). Susan is asked to draw a simple picture and describe it in Hungarian. Basil’s task is to ignore the words he doesn't understand and focus entirely on forming a mental image from her voice and gestures. Students complete a gap-fill task to summarize the instructions given to the participants, predicting whether Basil can successfully decode the message. Page 3: Student Participation (Stage 2) The class plays along with the experiment. Students listen to Susan’s description, close their eyes, trust their intuition, and draw the image that comes to their own minds. This hands-on activity tests their own ability to decode nonverbal cues and auditory tone without relying on linguistic meaning, creating a personal connection to the psychological process being studied. Page 4: The Reveal & Psychological Manipulation (Stage 3) The third stage reveals that Basil correctly drew an elephant! The class then reverse-engineers the "magic," analyzing Derren’s brilliant, subtle manipulation from the very beginning. They examine how the spiral-staircase venue mimicked a trunk, how Derren used his own trunk-like hand gestures while giving instructions, and how Susan embedded keywords like "India" and "Africa" into her description. This leads to a discussion on whether the success was due to pure intuition or clever psychological priming. Page 5: The Science of Suggestibility (Video/Text) The lesson transitions to its second media segment, exploring the science of "suggestibility" based on Erik Vance’s book Suggestible You. Students watch a short video or read a text explaining how expectation changes reality. They examine real-world examples, such as athletes performing better with a fake Gatorade label, students scoring higher with an "MIT pen," or wine tasting better from a fancy bottle. Students answer six targeted comprehension questions about how these psychological traits can be harnessed for health and performance. The class concludes with a mature, critical discussion on personal suggestibility. Students debate whether being "gullible" or "malleable" is a weakness or a strength. They explore who uses manipulation and influence professionally (e.g., politicians, marketers, police) and whether understanding these psychological tricks is a tool for self-protection or exploitation. The lesson wraps up by asking students if they would read Suggestible You to protect themselves or to learn how to influence others. 📄 Inside This Lesson Pack 💬 Core Discussion Prompts & Speaking Activities The 7-38-55 Rule: Do you believe that 93% of communication is nonverbal? Have you ever understood someone perfectly without sharing a common language? Intuition vs. Priming: Was Basil’s success due to pure intuition, or did Derren Brown’s subtle gestures and the room’s design give him the answer? Ethical Manipulation: Is it ethical for marketers or politicians to use suggestibility to influence people? Should we learn these tricks to protect ourselves or to use them? 🧠 Target Lexicon & Vocabulary Focus Body Language Terms: Posture, gestures, fidgeting, mirroring, gaze, glance, grimace, smirk. Psychology & Suggestibility: Suggestible, gullible, malleable, trait, perceive, harnessed, expectation, placebo effect. 📺 Multimedia & Authentic Materials Included Derren Brown Video (3 Stages): A targeted viewing task tracking the setup, student participation, and the final reveal of the "elephant" drawing, followed by an analysis of the hidden cues (spiral stairs, hand gestures, keywords). Suggestibility Text/Video: An exploration of real-world examples where expectation alters performance, including the Gatorade label study, the MIT pen test, and the fancy wine bottle experiment. ⚠️ Critical Thinking Extension Work Reverse-Engineering Magic: Students actively identify the specific environmental and verbal clues Derren Brown planted to guide Basil’s drawing, debating whether this counts as "mind reading" or just advanced observation. Real-World Application: Discussing how suggestibility can be used positively (e.g., boosting confidence in sports or exams) versus negatively (e.g., misleading advertising or political propaganda), and how to spot when it’s being used on you.

This lesson is designed to fit within a single 90 min session. The video component works well as homework before the live session.
