What's the "Meet Elara an AI influencer" ESL Lesson About?

A2-B1 · General English

Main Objectives

  • critically analyze AI-generated social media content and the life lessons embedded within viral influencer scenarios
  • master four distinct grammar structures: negative questions, past modals of possibility, the Second Conditional, and "make" collocations
  • apply practical English skills to discuss modern digital culture, hidden fees, ageism, and smart decision-making

About This Lesson

Page 1: The AI Influencer & Negative Questions The lesson introduces students to "Elara Whitmore," a 100% AI-generated Instagram character created by @lotzy.ai and @sibersorfcu. After watching the first video short, students analyze user comments discussing themes of ego, satisfaction, and "beauty with brains." This naturally transitions into the grammar focus: forming negative questions (e.g., Don’t you think...? Haven’t you used...? Shouldn’t you make...?). Students practice writing and asking their own negative questions to a partner. Page 2: Outsmarting Hidden Fees & Past Modals The second video short features Elara dealing with a Las Vegas hotel trying to add a surprise "facilities fee." Students read authentic user comments praising how she "outsmarted" the manager. This scenario introduces the grammar focus: past modals of possibility and impossibility (could have / could not have + past participle). Students analyze examples like "She could have used the pool, but she didn't," and then write 3-5 sentences about missed opportunities or alternative past actions in their own lives. Page 3: Ageism & The Second Conditional The third video short tackles superficiality, featuring a comment asking, "If I were forty years younger, would you marry me?" Students read reactions discussing age, money, and the term "spinster." This sparks a debate on modern relationship values and introduces the Second Conditional (If + Past Simple, ... would + verb). Students practice forming hypothetical questions (e.g., Would you marry an AI?) and ask their partners using the target structure. Page 4: Smart Choices & "Make" Collocations The final video short highlights Elara making a "good call" and asserting, "She makes coffee, I make the decisions." Students discuss the blurring lines between AI and reality. The lesson wraps up with a targeted vocabulary focus on collocations with the verb make (e.g., make progress, make a mistake, make an effort, make a complaint, make sense). Students match these collocations to their definitions and apply them in a personalized discussion about their own English learning journey and daily habits. 📄 Inside This Lesson Pack 💬 Core Discussion Prompts & Speaking Activities The AI Reality Check: Does knowing a video is 100% AI-generated change how you feel about the "life lesson" it teaches? Hidden Fees: Have you ever had to argue back-and-forth with a hotel or company to remove an unfair charge? How did you outsmart them? Superficiality vs. Substance: Do you agree with the comment that "it's how much money you've got in your bank account that matters" when it comes to relationships? Smart Choices: What was the last "good call" you made that saved you time, money, or stress? 🧠 Target Lexicon & Grammar Focus Page 1 Grammar: Negative Questions (Don’t you + verb, Haven’t you + past participle, Shouldn’t you + verb). Page 2 Grammar: Past Modals of Possibility (could have / could not have + past participle). Page 3 Grammar: Second Conditional for hypothetical present/future situations (If + Past Simple, ... would + verb). Page 4 Vocabulary: "Make" Collocations (make progress, make a mistake, make an effort, make a suggestion, make an excuse, make a complaint, make a joke, make a mess, make noise, make sense, make sure, make friends). Slang & Idioms: Hit a nerve, beauty with brains, outsmart, back-and-forth, good call, spinster. 📺 Multimedia & Authentic Materials Included 4 AI Video Shorts: Sequential viewing tasks featuring the AI character "Elara Whitmore," each presenting a distinct everyday scenario (ego/satisfaction, hotel fees, ageism, smart decisions). Authentic Social Media Comments: Real-world Instagram-style reactions used as reading comprehension and debate prompts on every page. ⚠️ Critical Thinking Extension Work The Ethics of AI Influencers: Debating whether AI characters that "teach life lessons" are harmless entertainment or deceptive manipulation. Grammar in Context: Students don't just memorize rules; they apply the Second Conditional to hypothetical AI scenarios (Would you date an AI if it were perfect?) and use "make" collocations to reflect on their own language learning progress (I need to make more of an effort to make fewer mistakes).

This lesson runs 60-90 min and can be split across two shorter sessions if needed. The video component works well as homework before the live session.
