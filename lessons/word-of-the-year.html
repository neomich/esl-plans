What's the "Word of The Year" ESL Lesson About?

B1-B2 · General English

Main Objectives

  • explore the origins of human language through four major linguistic theories (Bow-Wow, Pooh-Pooh, La-La, and Yo-He-Ho);
  • analyze the evolution of modern vocabulary through word formation processes like blending, clipping, and affixation;
  • evaluate the cultural significance of "Words of the Year" from major dictionaries (2020–2025) and their reflection of societal trends.

About This Lesson

Page 1: Language Evolution & Historical Audio The lesson opens by examining four historical theories about how language began, such as rhythmic chants for coordination, imitation of natural sounds, or automatic vocal responses to emotion. Students discuss which theory they find most plausible then watch and check. Next, they listen to a rare audio recording of Leo Tolstoy speaking about the "object of life" in English. This listening task gives students a unique opportunity to hear how the language sounded and was pronounced more than a hundred years ago, sparking a conversation about how language changes over time. Page 2: Word Formation & Words of the Year Students review three common ways new English words form: blending (e.g., textpectation), adding prefixes/suffixes (e.g., awesomeness), and clipping (e.g., blog). They then look at a chart of "Words of the Year" from 2020 to 2025 selected by major dictionaries (including terms like rizz, brat, permacrisis, vibe coding, and goblin mode). Students match these terms to their definitions, then listen to an audio podcast to verify their answers. The lesson concludes with a discussion using this targeted vocabulary to talk about modern digital habits, such as encountering rage bait or feeling parasocial connections online. 📄 Inside This Lesson Pack 💬 Core Discussion Prompts & Speaking Activities Language Origins: Which theory of language evolution (rhythmic chants, imitation, emotional responses) seems the most realistic to you? Historical Pronunciation: How different did English sound in the Tolstoy recording compared to modern English? What changes have you noticed in your own lifetime? Digital Culture: Do you ever feel like you are living in a permacrisis? What kind of online content gives you brain rot the fastest? 🧠 Target Lexicon & Vocabulary Focus Word Formation Methods: Blending (textpectation), Affixation (subprime, awesomeness), Clipping (blog, flu). Modern Dictionary Terms: Rizz, brat, permacrisis, vibe coding, goblin mode, rage bait, slop, parasocial, gaslighting, authentic, polarization. 🎧 Multimedia & Authentic Materials Included Page 1 Audio Task: A rare historical recording of Leo Tolstoy speaking English, used to illustrate language change and spark discussion on historical pronunciation. Page 2 Audio Task: A podcast segment where students verify their matches for the 2020–2025 Words of the Year from sources like Oxford, Merriam-Webster, Collins, and Cambridge. ⚠️ Critical Thinking Extension Work Homework Assignment: Students research and create their own "Words of the Year" list for 2026. They must select 3–5 terms that define the current cultural, technological, or social mood, write definitions for them, and prepare to present their choices in the next class. Vocabulary in Context: Applying modern slang and dictionary terms to real-life scenarios, evaluating whether these words accurately capture the current state of internet culture and society.

This lesson runs 60-90 min and can be split across two shorter sessions if needed. Both audio and video components are included, which also works well for homework-based or asynchronous practice.
