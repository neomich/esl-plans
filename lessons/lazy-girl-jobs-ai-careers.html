What's the "Lazy Girl Jobs & AI Careers" ESL Lesson About?

B1-B2 · Business English

Main Objectives

  • evaluate different job profiles and workplace vocabulary to discuss personal career priorities
  • analyze the "lazy girl job" trend through a targeted podcast, exploring themes of work-life balance and remote work
  • examine prompt engineering as a high-paying remote career via video comprehension and apply a 7-step framework for writing effective AI prompts

About This Lesson

Page 1: Job Profiles & Priorities Students begin by evaluating six distinct job profiles, ranging from a Social Media Assistant to an Emergency Room Nurse. They match key workplace vocabulary (e.g., appointment, emergency, background check, requirement) to their definitions and discuss the pros and cons of each role. This sets a grounded foundation for a conversation about what people truly value in their daily work. Page 2: The "Lazy Girl Job" Podcast The focus shifts to the viral "lazy girl job" trend. Students first review target phrasal verbs, then listen to a podcast discussing why this trend is growing. They fill in blanks with the target vocabulary, answer specific comprehension questions, and engage in a structured discussion. They debate whether this trend reflects actual laziness or a healthy demand for work-life balance, flexible hours, and the ability to completely switch off at the end of the day. The page concludes with students defining their own personal idea of the "Ultimate Lazy Girl Job." Page 3: The Ultimate "Lazy Girl Job" Video & Prompt Structure The lesson transitions to high-tech careers. Students examine a chart of high-paying AI roles (e.g., Chief AI Officer, AI Product Manager) and their salary ranges. They then watch a Wall Street Journal video investigating the "ultimate lazy girl job": prompt engineering. Students answer multiple-choice questions about the role's daily tasks, required skills, and salary potential. Following the video, they learn the 7 essential parts of a strong AI prompt (Role, Task, Context, Audience, Goal, Format, Constraints) and match these steps to practical examples, comparing weak prompts to stronger, more detailed ones. 📄 Inside This Lesson Pack 💬 Core Discussion Prompts & Speaking Activities The Trend Debate: Do you agree that a "lazy girl job" is not really about being lazy, but about setting healthy boundaries? Why or why not? Work-Life Boundaries: Is it realistic to draw a hard line between work and private life when you work from home? Should people be able to switch off completely at the end of the day? AI Career Viability: Would you apply for a job called "prompt engineer"? Do you think writing good prompts is a real professional skill, or will AI models eventually make this role unnecessary? 🧠 Target Lexicon & Vocabulary Focus Workplace Vocabulary: Appointment, emergency, stress, cashier, ID card, background check, working hours, requirement, daycare. Phrasal Verbs: Switch off, build up, stay up, draw the line, go back to. Prompt Engineering Terms: Role, Task, Context, Audience, Goal, Format, Constraints, large language models, datasets. 🎧 Multimedia & Authentic Materials Included Page 2 Podcast Task: A targeted listening exercise about the rise of the "lazy girl job," paired with a phrasal verb gap-fill and comprehension questions focusing on work-life priorities. Page 3 Video Task: A Wall Street Journal clip investigating prompt engineering, accompanied by a multiple-choice quiz covering the role's requirements, daily tasks, and salary expectations. Prompt Framework: A visual guide breaking down the 7 simple parts that change an AI's answer, requiring students to match weak prompts with stronger, well-structured alternatives. ⚠️ Critical Thinking Extension Work Personal Job Design: Students use the lesson's framework to draft their own definition of the "Ultimate Lazy Girl Job," balancing salary, stress levels, and remote flexibility. Prompt Optimization: Students take a vague, weak prompt (e.g., "Write something about productivity") and apply the 7-step structure to transform it into a clear, highly specific command that yields better results.

This lesson runs 60-90 min and can be split across two shorter sessions if needed. Both audio and video components are included, which also works well for homework-based or asynchronous practice.
